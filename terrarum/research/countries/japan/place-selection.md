# Japan: which places get their own page

Date: 2026-11-13

## Summary

Method: I read the owner databases first. That meant the Agency for Cultural Affairs' World Heritage list, and the national designation database (kunishitei.bunka.go.jp), queried for every National Treasure building (234 entries), every Special Historic Site (66) and every Special Place of Scenic Beauty (36). For demand I used the Japan Tourism Agency's 2025 inbound survey, which gives the share of foreign visitors who visited each prefecture. I then checked each candidate place against its owner's own site for status and story claims (temple, shrine, museum, park office or ministry). Each place was scored against the rule in the brief: (1) a top designation; (2) the only place for a story the atlas must tell; (3) among the places most visitors to the region go to. Viewpoints, bridges, sub-temples, markets and streets that sit inside a bigger place fold into it, or into the city page.

Headline: **29 place pages outside Kansai, down from 53, plus 15 of Kansai's 17 built pages, for 44 in all instead of 70.** Per region: Hokkaidō 4, Tōhoku 4, Kantō 5, Chūbu 4, Kansai 15, Chūgoku 3, Shikoku 4, Kyūshū and Okinawa 5. There are six new places the current plan lacks: Goryōkaku, Sannai-Maruyama, the Takata-Matsubara tsunami memorial park, Ishite-ji, Ritsurin Garden, and (as a swap inside Kansai) Hōryū-ji. Most of the 70 → 44 drop is folding, not deleting. Fifteen planned pages become sections of a parent page or city page.

What the evidence changed most:
- **Demand is very concentrated.** In 2025, 50.8% of foreign visitors went to Tokyo, 41.3% to Osaka and 29.7% to Kyoto. Every prefecture in Shikoku and most of Tōhoku was at 1.3% or below, and Shimane, home of Izumo Taisha, was the lowest at 0.1% [S-IVS]. Outside the big three, region pages have to stand on designation and story, not footfall. Picks are capped low there.
- **Some planned pages fail the test.** These are Iya Valley (no top designation, and Tokushima 0.2%), Shukkei-en (a plain Place of Scenic Beauty, not a Special one), Nakasu yatai, Ōhori Park, Kushida Shrine, Makishi Market, Shibuya Crossing and the 21st Century Museum.
- **Status changes.** Shuri Castle's main hall opens to the public on 23 Nov 2026 by advance booking [S-SHURI]. Dōgo Onsen Honkan finished its repairs in Dec 2024 [S-DOGO]. You can't enter Nagoya Castle's keep [S-NAGOYA]. Photography is banned inside the Chichu Art Museum [S-CHICHU].

## Notes on the selection rule

- **The rule works, with one change.** For regions outside Tokyo, Osaka and Kyoto, criterion 3 can only be tested at prefecture level: site counts are scattered and rarely comparable. So in practice criterion 3 is decided by the JTA prefecture shares, and criteria 1 and 2 decide the low-demand regions.
- **"National Treasure" should mean a building, not an object in a collection.** Many temples hold National Treasure objects, Shitennō-ji among them. Counting those would let almost any old temple pass.
- **A plain "Place of Scenic Beauty" (名勝) should not pass.** Only "Special" (特別名勝) should. This cuts Shukkei-en, Nara Park and Beppu.
